Manage access as people and responsibilities change.

Organise sites, manage physical and supported digital credentials, and give the right people responsibility for day-to-day operations.

What you can do

Sites with context.

Find a customer installation and review its users, keys, locks and related devices. Keep separate buildings organised within the supported site hierarchy.

Responsibilities with scope.

Give local administrators access to the areas they manage. Screens and actions depend on the person's permissions and subscription configuration.

Credential workflows.

Issue supported wallet credentials, manage physical cards and use supported temporary-access workflows. When access is no longer needed, revoke it and follow the delivery status.

An example: a contractor's visit.

A contractor needs access to a service area. The administrator selects the correct site and doors, issues the supported credential, and reviews its status. When the work ends, the administrator revokes access and checks the result.

  1. Selectthe site and the doors
  2. Issuethe supported credential
  3. Reviewits status
  4. Revokeand check the result

Support with evidence.

Review activity and audit records. Use supported device monitoring to identify connectivity problems and failed operations. Alerts require configuration; they do not replace an agreed support process.

When a reader is offline.

Some key changes are queued for later delivery. “Pending” means the change has not yet been confirmed on the device. Local credential behaviour depends on the device and configuration.

A revocation made while the reader is offline is not enforced until the reader confirms it.

Understand offline behaviour

Questions

Can I manage more than one site?

The Admin Panel organises customer installations as sites, called Entities, and supports site-scoped management. The available actions depend on account permissions and configured services.

What happens to a key change while a reader is offline?

Some key changes remain pending until the reader reconnects. Check the delivery result; an offline revocation should not be treated as already enforced.
