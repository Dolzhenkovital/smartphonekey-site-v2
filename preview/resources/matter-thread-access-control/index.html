Matter and Thread for access control

By the SmartphoneKey product team

Published 27 October 2026

Updated 27 October 2026

Product facts checked 26 October 2026

Matter describes what a connected device can do, such as switch on or report a temperature. Thread is an IP mesh network that Matter devices can use, and a Thread Border Router connects that mesh to your other IP networks. Every SmartphoneKey reader can act as a Thread Border Router and as a router in the mesh, without a separate firmware version. What a scenario can do depends on the capabilities each device reports.

Four roles that are easy to confuse

Matter and Thread roles
RoleWhat it doesWhat it does not mean
Matter deviceReports capabilities such as on/off, contact or temperatureThat every controller can use every function
Matter controllerCommissions devices and sends them commandsThat it forwards Thread traffic
Thread routerForwards traffic inside the Thread meshThat the mesh can reach the internet
Thread Border RouterConnects the Thread mesh to other IP networksThat it controls Matter devices

The roles of SmartphoneKey readers

Every SmartphoneKey reader can act as a Thread Border Router and as a Thread router, without a separate firmware version. Which reader carries the mesh's connection to the internet is decided for each site. Matter controller functions are confirmed for each project, and the roles of other devices depend on their own hardware and firmware.

An example topology

Example topology. Deployment depends on configuration.

  1. The site's internet connection, over Wi-Fi, Ethernet or a cellular network.
  2. A Thread Border Router, which links the Thread mesh to that network. Any SmartphoneKey reader can take this role.
  3. Thread routers, which pass traffic between devices in the mesh. Every SmartphoneKey reader can take this role too.
  4. Matter devices, such as sensors, lights and valves, each reporting its own capabilities.
  5. A Matter controller, which commissions the devices and runs the configured scenarios.

Capabilities decide what a scenario can do

A Matter label does not mean every device offers the same functions. A contact sensor reports open or closed; a light switches on or off; a leak sensor reports water; a valve opens or closes; a thermostat accepts a setpoint. A scenario can use only what the commissioned device reports and what your configuration supports.

One hub per scenario

Each scenario currently belongs to one hub and the devices commissioned to it. Plan scenarios around the hub that controls the devices they need.

Coverage and the route to the internet

A mesh depends on the roles of its devices, their placement and power, and the route to the internet. Coverage depends on the building, placement and radio conditions, so plan it for the site and check it after installation. When the route to the internet is lost, remote management waits for it to return; read what happens when a reader goes offline.

Questions to settle for your site

  • Which reader version and firmware will be installed?
  • Which readers will connect the mesh to the internet, and which other devices join the mesh?
  • How does the site reach the internet, and what should happen if that connection drops?
  • Which devices and capabilities does each scenario need, and which hub controls them?
