LTE or Wi-Fi: what fits your site?

By the SmartphoneKey product team

Published 27 October 2026

Updated 27 October 2026

Product facts checked 26 October 2026

Choose by the connection each door can rely on. A Wi-Fi reader suits doors with reliable Wi-Fi on a network you can configure as the installation guide describes. An LTE reader suits doors with reliable cellular coverage, where you can provide a SIM and service. Both readers work with the same platform, include the access controller and need power, compatible lock hardware and wiring.

On this page

  • What both readers have in common
  • What differs
  • What is decided for each project
  • Who manages the connection
  • Checklist before you choose
  • When to ask for a site review

What both readers have in common

  • They work with the same Admin Panel, so sites, people, keys and cards are managed the same way.
  • The access controller is built into the reader.
  • Each can act as a Thread Border Router and as a router in the Thread mesh, without a separate firmware version.
  • Each needs power, compatible lock hardware and the wiring described in the installation and connection guide.

What differs

The main difference is the connection each reader depends on.

How the LTE and Wi-Fi readers differ
What differsLTE readerWi-Fi reader
ConnectionCellular network (LTE)The site's Wi-Fi network
Check at each doorCellular coverage at the reader positionWi-Fi coverage at the reader position
You provideA SIM and cellular service for each readerNetwork access details and the settings in the installation guide
SuitsDoors with reliable cellular coverage, or sites where the local network cannot be usedDoors with reliable Wi-Fi on a network you can configure
To keep it workingKeep the SIM service activePlan for changes to the network name, password or equipment

What is decided for each project

  • Which doors get an LTE reader and which get a Wi-Fi reader.
  • Which reader version suits the region where it is installed. Ask us before ordering.
  • Which reader connects the Thread mesh to the internet, and what the site does if that connection drops.

Who manages the connection

With an LTE reader, someone provides the SIM and cellular service, keeps it active and checks coverage at the position where the reader will be mounted.

With a Wi-Fi reader, the reader joins the site's own network. The network owner provides the access details, keeps the network configured as the installation guide describes, and tells you before the network name, password or equipment change.

Checklist before you choose

For an LTE reader

  • Cellular coverage measured at each planned reader position, not just outside the building.
  • Who supplies the SIM and service, and in which region the reader is installed.
  • Who renews the service and who is told if it stops.

For a Wi-Fi reader

  • Wi-Fi coverage at each planned reader position.
  • Who owns and manages the network, and whether it restricts new devices.
  • How you will hear about changes to the network name, password or equipment.

When to ask for a site review

Ask us to review the site when coverage at a reader position is uncertain, when doors are in basements or behind heavy construction, when several buildings are planned together, or when a third party manages the network. Coverage depends on the building, placement and radio conditions, so it is checked on site rather than promised in advance.

Choose the connection that fits the site, then confirm the reader version for your region with us.
